Bringing ListWithMe Back to Life

After years off the App Store, I rebuilt a small app my wife and I missed using.

PublishedFebruary 24, 2026
Reading time3 minutes
Filed underiOS · SwiftUI · Building
Connected projectListWithMe ↗

Field note

Software can be worth rebuilding simply because you want it back.

Back in 2019, I built ListWithMe, a simple iMessage app for creating shared shopping lists. My wife and I used it constantly for grocery runs and weekend errands. It was nothing fancy, but it solved a real problem: quickly creating a list that both of us could see and edit right inside our conversation.

Then life happened. The app sat untouched as iOS evolved, and eventually Apple removed it from the App Store. I always meant to update it, but between work and other projects, it kept getting pushed to “someday.”

Well, someday finally arrived.

01A Complete Rebuild

Rather than patch the old UIKit codebase, I rebuilt ListWithMe from scratch using SwiftUI. The new version keeps the original simplicity while adding the details I had wanted for years.

  • Smart input understands “3 apples” or “milk x2” without slowing you down.
  • Categories keep a grocery run organized by aisle instead of creation order.
  • Activity makes it clear what changed when you are shopping together but apart.
“For my wife and me, it has become invisible infrastructure.”Personal rule 01 · Make what you want to use

02The Technical Side

The rewrite also gave me a reason to use a few newer iOS tools in a complete personal project: SwiftUI, the Observation framework, and Core Data backed by CloudKit.

03Why iMessage Apps Still Matter

There is something valuable about software that lives inside a conversation. You do not need to switch context, share another link, or coordinate which app to use. If you are already texting someone, the list is right there.

The code is open source if you want to poke around. More importantly, the app is useful to us again, which is the whole reason I wanted to rebuild it.
